# UNDERSTANDING: the roadmap, from the bit upward

> Generated by `cardmodel basics roadmap --write docs/UNDERSTANDING.md` from `cards/understanding` and from the
> results of the mastered levels' engines. Edit the cards or the engines, not this file.

The owner's direction (2026-11-17): master the most basic of basics first, completely, with the model's own card
logic, and expand from there to everything as understanding grows. This document is the map of what there is to
understand about computation on computers and in programs, ordered so that every level builds only on levels
below it, and the record of what is mastered.

A level is **mastered** only when it has an engine and every one of its mastery criteria computes true from that
engine's results; that is checked every time this file is generated and by `tests/test_bits.py`. **Next** marks
the frontier: levels not yet mastered whose prerequisites all are. Nothing in a status column is typed by hand.

### What mastering each level means

* **L00 The bit.** the two values are distinct and every comparison of them is decided · the rows of n bits are exactly 2^n and are enumerated in a fixed order · a function of bits is identified by its truth table and nothing else
* **L01 Every function of at most two bits.** all 16 functions are identified, with the inputs each depends on (constants and one-bit functions are found inside them) · the op cards' truth tables are observed by running them, never stated · every function has a smallest circuit over the op cards, re-run by the IR interpreter on every row · a single gate complete on its own is found, and every function has a proven-smallest circuit over it · all 65,536 sets of gates are decided complete or not, and Post's criterion agrees on every one · the laws of the op cards are found by enumeration and each is proved on every row
* **L02 Functions of n bits.** normal forms (sum of products, product of sums, XOR polynomial) are derived and checked on all rows · the multiplexer (IF_EXPR) is observed and Shannon expansion is found as a law · every one of the 256 three-bit functions has a proven-smallest formula and circuit; four bits by symmetry classes · deciding whether a function is ever true (satisfiability) is solved by search and by decision diagrams
* **L03 Adding bits.** half and full adder found as smallest multi-output circuits from L01/L02 pieces · ripple-carry addition of w bits built from full adders and checked on every input up to 8 bits · correctness for every width proved by induction on the width, not by sampling
* **L04 Words and encodings.** unsigned and two's-complement readings of w bits, and the ring of integers mod 2^w · WRAPU, WRAPS, the BIT_* and shift op cards rebuilt as circuits from L03 and checked against their cards on all 8-bit inputs · overflow and sign extension explained as laws
* **L05 Arithmetic on words.** SUB, MUL, DIV, REM, MOD and the CMP_* cards on fixed widths derived as circuits and checked exhaustively at 8 bits · flags (carry, zero, sign, overflow) derived, matching the ISA cards · costs of each construction measured (gates, depth)
* **L06 Approximate numbers.** fixed point and IEEE 754 binary32/64: fields, rounding, NaN and infinities derived from L05 · F32, BITS_F32 and F64_BITS cards checked against the derived encoding · error bounds stated as laws
* **L07 Characters.** code points, UTF-8 and UTF-16 as bit layouts derived and checked on every code point · STR_FROM_CODE and STR_ORD checked against the derived encoding
* **L08 State and feedback.** latch, flip-flop and register as circuits with feedback, and their behaviour over time · finite-state machines: a next-state function from L02 plus a state; equivalence and minimisation decided exactly
* **L09 Memory.** address decoding as an L02 function; read and write as state transitions · the IR's arrays, LOAD and store explained by this model
* **L10 The instruction machine.** fetch, decode and execute as one state transition · each instruction card's meaning derived from L05 circuits and checked against the card · a small ISA learned from execution traces
* **L11 Expressions and straight-line programs.** terms over the op cards evaluated, shared and costed; L01's circuits are the boolean case · laws over every sort found by enumeration as in L01, proved where the domain is finite
* **L12 Control flow.** branches and loops as control-flow graphs; loops as least fixed points · termination proved where it holds, reported where it is unknown
* **L13 Procedures and recursion.** calls, stack frames, recursion and calling conventions · recursion schemes and linear recurrences (funcalg) derived
* **L14 Data structures.** sequences, strings, records, trees, graphs and hash tables built from memory · value versus reference semantics
* **L15 Algorithms and their cost.** time and space measured and predicted; asymptotic classes · algorithm cards matched to measured cost
* **L16 Syntax.** regular languages as the finite-state machines of L08; context-free grammars and parsing · every frontend's grammar as cards
* **L17 Meaning and types.** operational and denotational meaning; types and soundness · the many-sorted signature of docs/FOUNDATIONS.md derived from these
* **L18 Translation and compilation.** lowering to machine code, register allocation, code generation · every translation checked equal in meaning
* **L19 Optimisation.** equivalence-preserving rewriting, cost models, superoptimisation (L01's smallest circuits are its smallest case)
* **L20 Operating systems and I/O.** processes, system calls, files, devices and interrupts as state machines
* **L21 Concurrency and distribution.** interleavings, races, locks, message passing and consensus, checked by exhaustive state search on small cases
* **L22 Computability.** Turing machines, lambda calculus, universality, the halting problem, Rice's theorem; Gödel numbering
* **L23 Complexity.** P, NP and reductions; satisfiability from L02 as the first NP-complete problem; circuit size from L01 and L02
* **L24 Information and learning.** information and description length; induction from behaviour; generalisation measured
* **L25 Proof and verification.** logic and proof systems; machine-checked proofs; L01's exhaustive proofs as the base case

## 2. Why this is the smallest piece

* **L00, the bit.** One value carries no information, so nothing can be computed with it. Two distinguishable values are the least that can be.
* **L01, functions of two bits.** The smallest level with a computation in it, and the only one small enough to finish: 16 objects, 4 rows, every search runs to the end. Functions of one bit cannot be combined into anything new (the model checks that no set of them is complete), so two bits is the smallest complete piece.
* Everything above is built from it: a word is many bits (L04), arithmetic is circuits of these functions (L03, L05), memory is these functions with feedback (L08), and an instruction machine is one big function of this kind applied again and again (L10).

### What the op cards are, by observation

Running every op card with a bool rank through the IR interpreter on every row gives: AND = AND, CMP_EQ = XNOR, CMP_NE = XOR, IF_EXPR = a function of 3 bits (table 11001010, the multiplexer; L02), NOT = NOT, OR = OR. 6 of the 16 functions have an op card; the other 10 are built below.

### Building everything from one gate

Of the 65,536 sets of two-bit gates, 64,800 build every function and 736 do not. The gates complete on their own are found, not given: **NOR and NAND**, and neither has an op card. There are 32 minimal complete sets (no member can be dropped): {NOR}, {NAND}, {NOT_X_AND_Y, NOT_X}, {NOT_X, X_AND_NOT_Y}, {NOT_X_AND_Y, NOT_Y}, {X_AND_NOT_Y, NOT_Y}, {NOT_X, AND}, {NOT_Y, AND}, {NOT_X_AND_Y, XNOR}, {X_AND_NOT_Y, XNOR}, {FALSE, X_IMPLIES_Y}, {NOT_X_AND_Y, X_IMPLIES_Y}, {NOT_X, X_IMPLIES_Y}, {X_AND_NOT_Y, X_IMPLIES_Y}, {NOT_Y, X_IMPLIES_Y}, {XOR, X_IMPLIES_Y}, {FALSE, Y_IMPLIES_X}, {NOT_X_AND_Y, Y_IMPLIES_X}, {NOT_X, Y_IMPLIES_X}, {X_AND_NOT_Y, Y_IMPLIES_X}, {NOT_Y, Y_IMPLIES_X}, {XOR, Y_IMPLIES_X}, {NOT_X, OR}, {NOT_Y, OR}, {NOT_X_AND_Y, TRUE}, {X_AND_NOT_Y, TRUE}, {FALSE, AND, XNOR}, {XOR, AND, XNOR}, {FALSE, XNOR, OR}, {XOR, XNOR, OR}, {XOR, AND, TRUE}, {XOR, OR, TRUE}.

No set of functions that depend on at most one input is complete (confirmed over all sets), which is why two bits is the smallest complete piece.

The searches are exhaustive by gate count, so each size in the table is a minimum. Programs tried per gate count: op cards: 1, 10, 134; NOR: 1, 3, 13, 75, 509, 4,038; NAND: 1, 3, 13, 75, 509, 4,038.
For example XNOR needs 5 NAND gates (`g1 = NAND(x, x); g2 = NAND(x, y); g3 = NAND(y, y); g4 = NAND(g1, g3); g5 = NAND(g2, g4)`), and every program with fewer was tried and failed.

### Why a set of gates is incomplete

Ordering all 26 closed sets of two-bit functions, the model finds exactly 4 maximal incomplete ones. A set of gates is complete exactly when it is inside none of them:

* {FALSE, NOT_X_AND_Y, X_AND_NOT_Y, XOR, AND, Y, X, OR}: the functions that give FALSE when every input is FALSE
* {FALSE, NOT_X, NOT_Y, XOR, XNOR, Y, X, TRUE}: the functions that are an XOR of inputs and a constant (affine)
* {FALSE, AND, Y, X, OR, TRUE}: the functions that never fall when an input rises (monotone)
* {AND, XNOR, Y, X_IMPLIES_Y, X, Y_IMPLIES_X, OR, TRUE}: the functions that give TRUE when every input is TRUE

These are Post's classes (1941), found here as structure, not taken from the theorem. Post names five; for gates of two inputs only 4 are distinct (the self-dual functions lie inside the affine ones: every two-input self-dual function is a projection or a negation). Checked independently against Post's criterion computed from its definitions: 0 disagreements in 65,536 sets.

## 4. The next step: L02

The search code takes the number of inputs as a parameter, so L02 starts from it. Already: every one of the 256 functions of three bits has a smallest formula over the 16 two-bit gates (256 found, the largest needs 4 gates). The full adder, the first piece of arithmetic (L03), falls out: sum `XOR(x, XOR(y, z))` (2 gates) and carry `XNOR(x, NAND(XOR(x, y), XOR(x, z)))` (4 gates). These are formulas (no sharing); L02's criteria (smallest circuits, normal forms, the multiplexer, satisfiability) are what remains.

## 5. Honest limits

* L00 and L01 are mastered in the sense their criteria state: complete for functions of at most two bits, in gate count. Depth, fan-out and real hardware cost are not modelled yet.
* Laws are complete up to 3 gates per side in three variables; larger laws exist and are found by raising the size, at the cost of time.
* The kept laws are a basis for the enumeration's rewriting, not a proven-minimal axiom system.
* The roadmap is a map: it orders what we know of, and it will grow. A level is mastered only by an engine whose criteria compute true; related work elsewhere in the repository does not count.
